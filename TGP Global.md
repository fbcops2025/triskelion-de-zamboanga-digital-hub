# Tau Gamma Phi Global: Publication Policy and Research Audit

## Publication decision

**Import as attributed external evidence, not as unqualified canonical governance.**

A large portion of the Tau Gamma Phi Global and Western Overseas material is useful to the national archive. We should preserve its facts, dates, structure, source URLs, and terminology; rewrite explanatory copy in this platform's editorial voice; and clearly identify the original publisher. We should not reproduce the Global site page-for-page or present one published governance model as settled authority for every Tau Gamma Phi body.

This preserves a valuable record while respecting separate councils, current-office uncertainty, source provenance, and media rights.

## Required public framing

Do not publish the following as an unsupported universal claim:

> This is the official organizational structure of all Tau Gamma Phi.

Use this source-aware introduction instead:

> **Tau Gamma Phi Global Structure**<br>
> Tau Gamma Phi Global materials describe a worldwide governance framework connecting Chapters, Areas, Clusters, and a Global Board. This section preserves that structure, its terminology, and its relationship to overseas councils as documented by Tau Gamma Phi Global and Western Overseas sources.

The Global Structure page says the Constitution was submitted for ratification by council leadership and separately says participation or signatures without council authority did not necessarily constitute ratification. It also contains an unresolved transition-timeline conflict: one answer refers to a temporary board through May 2020, while later answers discuss a May 2022 election and service until elected members were sworn in. Preserve both as source records. Do not resolve or rewrite the discrepancy as institutional fact without a dated amendment history or ratification record. [1]

## Media and documents: do not re-upload by default

Do **not** download and republish third-party photographs, portraits, logos, graphics, gallery images, souvenir-program pages, or PDFs merely because they are publicly visible. Public availability is not permission to redistribute.

Until rights are clear, preserve only:

- title, description, date, and source URL;
- related council, person, event, and verification status; and
- a thumbnail only where the license, permission, or platform terms allow it.

For third-party video, prefer linking to or embedding the original published player when its use is permitted. Do not re-upload a copy without rights clearance.

# Tau Gamma Phi Global Structure: Deep Research Audit and Knowledge-Base Package

## Executive Findings

I completed a structured crawl and cross-check of the **Tau Gamma Phi Global Structure** material centered on the supplied `about-global-structure` page, together with the site's current-info page, Western Overseas directory, leadership pages, bulletin, TDAP material, gallery, video references, event records, registration portal, and external corroborating sources.

The strongest conclusion is that `taugammaphi.info` is valuable as an **organization-controlled source for the Tau Gamma Phi Global / Western Overseas governance narrative**, but its contents should not be imported into the national knowledge base as an unqualified universal governance record.

The site explicitly describes itself as an official Tau Gamma Phi / Tau Gamma Phi Global West Overseas website, identifies Western Overseas as its operational context, and says the website is managed by Blaise Juliano, whom it identifies as a member associated with Tau Gamma Phi, Western Overseas, and the Global Structure. citeturn16search1turn18view2turn18view3

The Global Structure page contains a detailed governance model. It describes:

**Chapter → Area → Cluster → Global Board**

It defines an **Area** as a chartered city, province, or country and a **Cluster** as a Region or grouping of countries. It lists **12 clusters**, describes a **15-member Global Board**, and assigns 12 seats to cluster representatives plus three designated seats. citeturn16search2turn17search0

I have therefore classified the safest ingestible concept as:

> **Tau Gamma Phi Global governance model, as published by the Western Overseas site**

rather than immediately calling it:

> “the official national organizational structure of Tau Gamma Phi.”

That distinction matters because the source itself says the Constitution was submitted for ratification by council leadership, while also cautioning that participation in meetings or signatures without a council mandate were not necessarily ratification. citeturn16search2

The package contains **38 files**, including the Markdown knowledge-base structure, master audit, claim registry, source registry, media inventory, links, people records, individual video records, and selected individual media records.

## Global Structure and Governance Findings

The Global Constitution Q&A is unusually detailed and potentially very useful for the national archive. It does not describe the organization as a simple National → Region → Council → Chapter hierarchy. Instead, it says chapters form **Areas**, Areas form **Clusters**, and Clusters elect or supply representation to the Global Board. It also says school-based chapters continue under their Area Councils alongside community chapters. citeturn16search2

The source describes chapter officers including the Grand Triskelion, Master Keeper of the Chest, Master Keeper of the Scrolls, Auditor, Election Commissioner, and Judge. It then describes an indirect election ladder moving from chapter-level participation through Area and Cluster governance. citeturn16search2

The source describes the Global Board as having **15 members**: twelve cluster representatives and three designated members. The designated seats are described as representing service-sector/alumni organizations, the UP Mother Chapter, and a Founding Fathers appointee. citeturn16search2

One of the most important audit discoveries is an **internal chronology conflict**. One Q&A answer says that the Transitory Global Board would temporarily serve until **May 2020**. Later answers say the first regular election would take place on the second Monday of **May 2022** and that the Transitory Global Board would remain until the elected board members were sworn in. citeturn16search2turn17search0

I preserved both claims rather than “correcting” either one. The national archive should obtain a **dated/versioned Constitution and amendment history** before turning this transition period into public historical narrative.

The source also says existing council constitutions, bylaws, and rules remain in place, and it explicitly does not define a universal city/municipal layer. That is significant for the future national data model: the external Global Structure material itself does not justify forcing every council into one rigid hierarchy. citeturn16search2

The site's current-info page goes further and says Western Overseas works through a “ratified Global Constitution,” “ratified Global Bylaws and Implementing Guidelines,” while also making the much more consequential claim that ties with the National Executive Council were severed in connection with hazing-related deaths. Those assertions are preserved in the claim registry, but I classified the ratification and NEC-related assertions as **NEEDS VERIFICATION** until signed resolutions, ratification records, or equivalent primary documents are available. citeturn16search1

For legal context, Republic Act No. 11053 is independently verifiable through the Supreme Court E-Library. The law was approved on **June 29, 2018**, is titled the Anti-Hazing Act of 2018, and prohibits hazing in school-based and community-based fraternities and organizations. That verifies the legal context, but it does **not** independently prove any particular fraternity body's compliance with the law. citeturn23search0turn23search1

## Recommended Treatment in the National Website

I recommend importing the entire package under **external evidence**, not directly replacing your canonical national records.

That fits the source-aware architecture already being developed for the national project, where factual records, verification status, and provenance are kept distinct from polished public narrative. fileciteturn0file0

For example, instead of publishing:

> “Tau Gamma Phi is officially structured as Chapters, Areas, Clusters, and the Global Board.”

I recommend:

### Global Structure and Overseas Governance

**Tau Gamma Phi Global materials published by the Western Overseas site describe a governance model that moves from Chapters to Areas, from Areas to Clusters, and from Clusters to a Global Board.**

The source lists **12 clusters** and describes a **15-seat Global Board**, with 12 representatives drawn from the clusters and three designated seats. It also states that existing council constitutions and bylaws remain in place. citeturn16search2

Because public records reflect more than one description of national and global governance, the national archive should identify this specifically as the **Tau Gamma Phi Global structure described by its Western Overseas source** until current national authority and ratification are documented.

For the council directory, I recommend:

### Western Overseas Councils and Chapters

The Western Overseas directory publishes a network of registered units across the United States, Canada, Europe, South America, and the Caribbean. Its records include bodies described as chapters, regional councils, and other organizations, so each entry is preserved under the name and classification used by the original source while its current charter and leadership are reviewed. citeturn18view2

### Western Overseas Leadership

A Western Overseas governor message dated **September 4, 2025** identifies **Robert “Bert” Reyes** as Governor of the TGPG West Overseas Cluster. The same message recognizes **Loy Loyola** and **Blaise Juliano** as predecessor governors. citeturn18view3
