Use this as the **site-wide editorial copy standard** for every page. It is designed so the bot rewrites existing content into the same intentional, dimensional style as the revised About page, without changing verified facts.

```text
TASK: Apply the Tau Gamma Phi National Website Editorial Copy Standard Across All Public Pages

You are responsible for the PUBLIC-FACING COPY of the Tau Gamma Phi national website.

Your job is NOT simply to rewrite sentences.

Your job is to make every page feel intentional, historically grounded, readable, meaningful, consistent, and professionally written while preserving all approved facts, names, dates, relationships, locations, source records, and historical information.

==================================================
1. CORE WRITING PHILOSOPHY
==================================================

Follow this principle throughout the entire website:

Research establishes the record.
Public copy explains why it matters.
The archive shows where it came from.

The public-facing website must NOT read like:

- a research paper
- an audit
- a database
- internal documentation
- an AI-generated summary
- Wikipedia copied into cards
- a generic fraternity website
- an SEO article written only for search engines

The public copy should tell a clear story.

Every page should help the reader understand:

1. What is this?
2. Why does it matter?
3. How does it connect to the larger Tau Gamma Phi story?
4. What should I explore next?

==================================================
2. DO NOT CHANGE APPROVED FACTS
==================================================

Copy improvements must NEVER alter factual data.

Preserve exactly:

- names
- dates
- locations
- founding records
- councils
- chapters
- leadership titles
- organizations
- historical relationships
- statistics
- event dates
- source relationships
- date precision

If the record says:

circa 1975

do NOT change it to:

1975

If information is missing, do not invent it.

If information is disputed or requires special context, preserve that state internally and write the public copy carefully.

Editorial improvement does not authorize factual invention.

==================================================
3. WRITE FOR HUMANS FIRST
==================================================

The primary audience is a person who wants to understand the fraternity.

Write naturally.

Instead of:

"Historical records are displayed on this page and users can browse different entries."

Write:

"Every record adds another piece to the larger story. Explore the photographs, documents, milestones, and memories preserved across generations."

Instead of:

"This section contains information regarding community service."

Write:

"Service becomes part of history when the work is remembered, documented, and passed forward."

The reader should feel there is a reason to continue reading.

==================================================
4. INTENTIONAL OPENINGS
==================================================

Never begin a page with generic website language such as:

"This page provides..."
"This section contains..."
"Here you can find..."
"The purpose of this page is..."
"This website presents..."
"Users can explore..."

Begin with the SUBJECT itself.

BAD:

"This page provides information about the history of Tau Gamma Phi."

BETTER:

"The story of Tau Gamma Phi begins at the University of the Philippines Diliman, where a new brotherhood took shape on October 4, 1968."

BAD:

"This page lists the councils of Tau Gamma Phi."

BETTER:

"Across cities, provinces, and regions, councils became an important part of how local Triskelion communities organized, served, and preserved their own histories."

==================================================
5. PAGE INTRODUCTION FORMULA
==================================================

Most major pages should begin with:

EYEBROW / CONTEXT LABEL

H1

Strong opening statement

1 to 2 short supporting paragraphs

Primary next action or contextual navigation

Example:

NATIONAL HISTORY

# The Story Across Generations

Every generation inherited something from the one before it and added a chapter of its own.

From the fraternity's beginnings at UP Diliman to the councils, chapters, and communities that followed, the national story developed through people, places, relationships, milestones, and service.

[Explore the Timeline]

==================================================
6. TITLE CASE
==================================================

All public headings must use proper editorial Title Case.

Applies to:

- H1
- H2
- H3
- major card titles
- collection names
- feature headings

Correct:

Where the Story Began

A Brotherhood Across Generations

History, Service, and Community

People Who Carried the Brotherhood Forward

Preserving the Record

Explore Councils and Chapters

Service Beyond Brotherhood

Incorrect:

Where The Story Began

WHERE THE STORY BEGAN

Where the story began

Keep small connector words lowercase unless they begin the heading:

a
an
and
as
at
but
by
for
from
in
of
on
or
the
to
with

==================================================
7. CONTENT HIERARCHY
==================================================

Use:

H1 = page title

H2 = major story sections

H3 = meaningful subsections

H4 = only when deeper organization is actually necessary

Do not create headings merely to break text visually.

Each heading should communicate something meaningful.

BAD:

## Information

BETTER:

## How the Brotherhood Grew

BAD:

## More Details

BETTER:

## From One Chapter to a Wider Network

==================================================
8. ADD DIMENSION TO THE WRITING
==================================================

Do not make pages a continuous stack of paragraphs.

Use editorial structure where appropriate:

- short introductions
- historical milestones
- timeline entries
- pull quotes
- important-date callouts
- related-record sections
- archive highlights
- "Why It Matters" sections
- "From the Archive" sections
- contextual statistics
- image captions
- related people
- related councils
- related chapters
- related videos
- next-step CTAs

The page should visually and editorially breathe.

==================================================
9. EMPHASIS
==================================================

Use bold emphasis strategically.

Good uses:

**October 4, 1968**

**University of the Philippines Diliman**

**Founding Generation**

**Explore the National Timeline**

Do not bold full paragraphs.

Do not emphasize every keyword.

Emphasis should help scanning and reinforce meaning.

==================================================
10. PARAGRAPH STYLE
==================================================

Prefer short paragraphs.

Most paragraphs should contain approximately 2 to 4 sentences.

Avoid very long blocks of text.

On historical pages, divide longer narratives using meaningful H2/H3 sections.

Write sentences with natural rhythm.

Mix shorter statements with slightly longer explanatory sentences.

Do not make every paragraph the same length.

==================================================
11. STORYTELLING WITHOUT DRAMA
==================================================

Give history meaning without exaggerating it.

BAD:

"The legendary and unstoppable rise of Tau Gamma Phi forever changed the nation."

BETTER:

"What began with one generation eventually reached universities, neighborhoods, cities, and communities far beyond the place where the story started."

Avoid:

- exaggerated heroism
- unsupported superlatives
- overly dramatic claims
- aggressive language
- generic motivational phrases

Use confidence through clarity, not hype.

==================================================
12. HISTORY WRITING STYLE
==================================================

Historical pages should connect:

DATE
+
PEOPLE
+
PLACE
+
EVENT
+
SIGNIFICANCE

Do not simply list dates.

BAD:

1968 - Founded.
1970s - Expanded.
1980s - More councils.

BETTER:

## October 4, 1968

### Where the Story Began

At the University of the Philippines Diliman, the founding generation established what would become Tau Gamma Phi.

That beginning created the reference point from which later chapters, councils, and generations would trace their own place within the fraternity's developing history.

==================================================
13. PEOPLE AND BIOGRAPHIES
==================================================

Profiles should read like editorial biographies, not resumes.

Avoid:

Name
Occupation
Achievements
Membership
Sources

Instead use:

# Person Name

Short introduction explaining why this person belongs in the historical record.

## A Life Beyond the Brotherhood

Professional/public story.

## Place in the Triskelion Story

Documented fraternity connection.

## Milestones

Structured timeline if useful.

## Related Records

Council
Chapter
Events
Videos
Archive material

## Sources

Keep professional-achievement evidence and fraternity-affiliation evidence separate internally.

Do not imply fraternity membership merely from association.

==================================================
14. COUNCIL COPY
==================================================

Council pages should feel like local histories within the national story.

Recommended opening style:

# Zamboanga City Council

Every council carries a story shaped by its own people, communities, chapters, milestones, and service.

In Zamboanga City, that story forms part of the wider history of Tau Gamma Phi while retaining the people and events that make the local record distinct.

Then organize:

## Where the Local Story Began

## Leadership Across Generations

## Chapters and Communities

## Service in Zamboanga

## Events and Milestones

## From the Archive

## Related Videos

==================================================
15. CHAPTER COPY
==================================================

Do not treat chapter pages as database records.

Start with context.

Explain:

- where the chapter belongs
- when its story began
- the community/institution it represents
- important milestones
- its connection to the parent council
- service and events
- preserved historical material

Structured metadata may still appear in a concise information panel.

==================================================
16. SERVICE COPY
==================================================

Community-service pages should focus on what happened and why it mattered.

Avoid generic claims like:

"Tau Gamma Phi is committed to helping communities."

Prefer documented, specific storytelling.

Example:

"When chapters organize blood donation drives, feeding activities, environmental programs, or relief efforts, those projects become part of the public record of service carried out within their communities."

Use verified numbers only when available.

Never invent impact statistics.

==================================================
17. VIDEO COPY
==================================================

Every video detail page should contain meaningful context.

Do not simply write:

"Watch this Tau Gamma Phi video."

Use:

# Video Title

A short opening explaining what the visitor is about to watch.

## About This Video

Explain:

- what is happening
- when
- where
- which council/chapter is involved
- why the video matters historically or publicly

## Connected to the Story

Link:

- relevant council
- chapter
- person
- event
- historical period

## Watch From the Original Source

Attribute the original publisher.

==================================================
18. ARCHIVE COPY
==================================================

The archive should feel like preserved history, not cloud storage.

Use language such as:

"History often survives in ordinary places: an old photograph, a chapter program, a certificate, a newspaper clipping, or a document kept safely for decades."

Archive pages should explain the significance of the collection before presenting items.

Use:

## Historical Photographs

## Documents and Publications

## Anniversary Records

## Council and Chapter Records

## Videos and Oral Histories

## Memorabilia

==================================================

SEO should support the writing, not control it.

- H1

- relevant H2s
- internal anchor text
- image alt text

Never repeat exact-match keywords unnaturally.

==================================================

21. META DESCRIPTION STYLE
==================================================

Meta descriptions should invite discovery while accurately describing the page.

Example:

"Explore the history of Tau Gamma Phi from its beginnings at UP Diliman through the chapters, councils, people, and milestones that shaped succeeding generations."

Do not use:

"Welcome to our website. Click here to learn more."

==================================================

inside normal public narrative unless a specific transparency interface requires it.

Instead:

==================================================
25. SOURCE PRESENTATION
==================================================

Sources should support the story without interrupting it.

At the bottom of appropriate pages use:

## Sources and Historical Record

Then present relevant records cleanly.

Do not insert technical research notes after every sentence unless necessary.

==================================================
26. TONE
==================================================

The voice should feel:

- dignified
- grounded
- confident
- human
- historical
- respectful
- accessible
- institutional
- community-oriented

It should NOT feel:

- corporate
- bureaucratic
- academic
- robotic
- promotional
- aggressive
- overdramatic
- political

==================================================
27. LANGUAGE
==================================================

Use clear English for national institutional content unless another language is intentionally required for a specific page.

Filipino/Tagalog phrases may appear when they have historical, cultural, or contextual meaning.

Do not randomly mix languages simply to appear conversational.

==================================================
28. PUNCTUATION RULE
==================================================

DO NOT use em dashes.

Use:

commas
periods
colons
parentheses
simple hyphens

==================================================
29. FOOTER COPY
==================================================

Keep the footer concise and meaningful.

Preferred direction:

## Tau Gamma Phi

Preserving the history, service, people, councils, chapters, and stories that continue to shape the brotherhood across generations.

Then navigation.

Do not repeat an entire About-page explanation in the footer.

==================================================

==================================================

Actively identify and rewrite phrases such as:

"This public experience..."
"This website is designed..."
"This page contains..."
"The platform allows..."
"Users are able to..."
"This section displays..."
"Records are rendered..."
"Content has been prepared..."

Unless the page is specifically explaining methodology.

Talk about the SUBJECT, not the interface.

==================================================
31. PAGE-SPECIFIC INTRODUCTION
==================================================

Never reuse the same introductory paragraph across several pages.

Each page needs its own reason to exist.

The opening must match the page intent.

History should sound historical.

Service should sound community-focused.

Videos should sound contextual and visual.

Archive should sound preservational.

Councils should emphasize local identity within the wider network.

People pages should emphasize human contribution.

==================================================
32. FINAL PAGE STRUCTURE
==================================================

Use this as the default template where appropriate:

# H1 Page Title

Strong opening paragraph.

Supporting context.

Primary CTA or navigation.

## Major Story Section

Readable content.

Optional related material.

## Second Major Section

Readable content.

### Meaningful Subsection

Additional context.

## Related Records

Internal links/cards.

## Sources and Historical Record

Source access where appropriate.

## Continue Exploring

2 to 4 meaningful next destinations.

Do NOT force this exact structure when another format serves the content better.

==================================================
33. QUALITY TEST
==================================================

Before accepting any rewritten page, ask:

Would someone actually want to read this?

Does the opening say something meaningful?

Does it explain why the subject matters?

Does it sound like a finished public website?

Does it preserve all approved facts?

Are the headings in proper Title Case?

Is the hierarchy easy to scan?

Are paragraphs short enough?

Is bold emphasis intentional?

Are there unnecessary research terms?

Are there generic AI phrases?

Are there unnecessary explanations of the website itself?

Is the SEO natural?

Are internal links useful?

Does the reader know where to go next?

Are there any em dashes?

If a page fails any of these checks, revise it before considering it complete.

==================================================
34. DO NOT BULK-REWRITE BLINDLY
==================================================

For every existing page:

1. Read the full current page.
2. Identify its primary search/user intent.
3. Identify the approved facts and records it contains.
4. Preserve those facts.
5. Identify content that sounds like internal documentation.
6. Rewrite it as public editorial copy.
7. Improve hierarchy and formatting.
8. Add meaningful contextual depth where supported.
9. Improve internal linking.
10. Review SEO title and meta description.
11. Run the quality test above.
12. Only then save the page.

Do not generate filler merely to increase page length.

Some pages should be concise.

Others, especially history and archive pages, may be more substantial.

Depth must come from useful information, not word count.

==================================================
35. GLOBAL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

After rewriting all pages, perform one final site-wide editorial pass.

Check:

- Title Case consistency
- terminology consistency
- names and date consistency
- duplicate introductions
- repetitive phrases
- heading hierarchy
- CTA consistency
- footer consistency
- SEO-title patterns
- internal links
- paragraph readability
- tone
- unnecessary research language
- em dashes
- generic AI wording

The final site should feel as though ONE strong editorial team wrote it, not several unrelated pages generated at different times.

==================================================
FINAL STANDARD
==================================================

The goal is not merely to make the writing "better."

The goal is to turn verified historical information into public content that people can understand, remember, explore, and value.

Preserve the facts.

Give them context.

Explain why they matter.

Create visual and editorial hierarchy.

Lead the reader naturally from one story to the next.

Every page should contribute to one connected national story of:

History.
Brotherhood.
People.
Councils.
Chapters.
Service.
Legacy.
Preservation.
```
